# Writing Marketing Copy

Manual-invocation only skill. Only apply this when explicitly asked to write or review website copy, landing pages, ads, or other marketing/promotional copy.

## Out of scope

Do not apply this skill to:
- Blog posts
- Internal product copy (in-app strings, product UI, docs, internal memos)

If asked to review something in one of these categories, say so and skip the review rather than applying these principles.

## Workflow

1. Read `reference.md` in this skill folder for the full distilled principles (headlines, appeals, body copy, web scannability, offers/CTAs, images, testing, and stage-appropriate caveats for an early-stage startup).
2. When reviewing existing copy: go section by section (headline, opening paragraph, body, offer/CTA, images) and flag specific violations, citing the specific principle (e.g. "appeal isn't in the headline, only in the body" or "round number, use the precise figure instead"). Don't just say "this is weak," name the exact fix.
3. When writing new copy: draft the headline last if the key selling point isn't obvious yet (write copy first to find it, then write the headline, then rewrite the opening to flow from that headline). Produce multiple headline options (aim for several distinct angles: self-interest, news, curiosity+benefit), not just one.
4. Always apply the web-scannability checklist (Section 6 of `reference.md`) to anything meant to be read on a page: subheads, bullets, bold benefits, short paragraphs, captioned images.
5. Apply the Section 11 reality check: at Ito's current stage, favor clarity and covering the basics over chasing "perfect," and don't claim a variant "wins" without real traffic behind it. A two-version gut check (show two, ask which and why) is a reasonable substitute for formal A/B testing at low volume.

## Quick checklist (highest-leverage items)

- Headline carries the specific appeal/benefit, not just the body copy.
- Self-interest and/or news beat curiosity alone; if using curiosity, pair it with an implied benefit.
- Specific numbers, not round ones.
- Opening paragraph continues the headline's exact thought and states the benefit immediately.
- Body copy proves claims with facts, not adjectives.
- At least one scannable element (subhead, bullet, bold benefit) visible at any scroll position.
- Every image has a caption that carries the selling point.
- One clear, specific call to action; no vague urgency.
- When choosing between two drafts, show both and ask which and why, don't show one and ask "is this good?"

See `reference.md` for the full detail behind every item above, including the complete headline-formula list, the offer/CTA checklist, copy styles to use/avoid, and layout guidance.
